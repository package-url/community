## Notes

- Meeting minutes are being kept and will be published, and the meeting will be recorded with Google Meet video and Gemini "note-taking".
- Our code of conduct (link in agenda above) applies to this meeting.
- Introductions.
- Summary
  - Steve Springett clarified the process for updating Ecma documents and related websites and repositories.
  - The group reviewed progress and constraints for completing the VERS standard.
  - A provisional registration process for new/private VERS types was proposed to allow name reservation without requiring immediate full specification complexity.
  - Michael Herzog explained the recent restructuring of PURL documentation in the purl-spec repository.
  - Philippe Ombredanne proposed moving the univers library from AboutCode to the PURL organization.
  - Jaime Rodríguez-Guerra provided updates on the 'conda' PURL type and Python Packaging.
  - The group explored the concept of a new PURL type for "virtual requirements" such as interfaces or compilers. They agreed to separate virtual dependencies from the software-id initiative for clarity.
  - The group discussed how to accommodate private VERS types or namespaces. They agreed to start with a lightweight provisional registration process for new VERS types.
  - The group discussed challenges with adding PURL information to CVE records.
  - Development resources are now concentrated on registering the new 'software-id' and 'git' PURL types to address widespread industry use cases.
  - The group reviewed ongoing complications with the Authorized Data Provider model for CVE records.
- **Meeting Initiation and Administrative Procedures**: Philippe Ombredanne opened the meeting with a reminder to adhere to the code of conduct and noted the session is being recorded. Following a roll call, participants discussed agenda items, including updates on PURL and VERS.
- **EC Markup Updates**: Michael Herzog and Steve Springett discussed EC markup documentation. Steve Springett confirmed that the approach for updating documentation is to modify existing documents on the main branch, which should lower the barrier for participation.
- **Website and GitHub.io Inquiries**: Steve Springett clarified that there is no action required from Michael Herzog regarding a previous ask about websites and GitHub.io repositories. The request was specifically to implement these structures for all ratified specifications, including PURL (427), Cyclone (424), and Common Life Cycle Enumeration (428). Michael Herzog noted that while a pull request exists for 427, they lack the permissions to finalize it and plan to address this.
- **VERS Updates**: Immanuel Kunz outlined agenda items for VERS, including an open pull request regarding the implementation of canonical requirements, a requirement for URL quoting, and a discussion on private namespaces.
- **VERS Progress and Resource Constraints**: Jonatan Männchen reported that progress on VERS has been delayed due to competing priorities with CVS work. They requested assistance to help move the effort forward. Michael Herzog, despite noting that some of the technical work is complex, agreed to review the material and provide assistance during their current focus on VERS development.
- **software-id and Python Packaging**: Jaime Rodríguez-Guerra raised questions regarding the new software-id PURL type and the PURL registry (https://github.com/package-url/purl-registry) in the context of pending PEP 725 and 804, seeking to align these efforts with Python packaging needs.
- **Git PURL Type Documentation**: Jon Moroney provided an update on the first draft of the 'git' PURL type human review documentation. Michael Herzog noted that the draft looks good and requested that Jon address a final question regarding duplicate test cases before the pull request can be merged.
- **Package-URL Security**: Philippe Ombredanne reported a significant increase in downloads for the packageurl-python library, reaching approximately 1.3 million per day and 31 million for the previous month. They suggested this might be the result of Continuous Integration (CI) activity.
- **PURL Documentation Restructuring**: Michael Herzog discussed a major restructuring of PURL documentation, moving generated files into `docs/types/definitions` and establishing a `docs/types/notes` folder for decision-making context and technical rationale. They emphasized the importance of capturing this information to reduce the cognitive load on new community members who might otherwise have to read through entire pull request histories.
- **Personall Affiliation Update**: Jon Moroney announced that they are no longer at Red Hat and have joined a new company called Socket.
- **univers Library Migration**: Philippe Ombredanne proposed moving the univers library to the PURL organization to make it easier for people to find. They intend to reach out to other organizations to ensure links to other package implementations, such as those in Rust, Go, and Java, are documented.
- **Maintenance Support**: Jaime Rodríguez-Guerra offered to help with the maintenance of the univers library, noting they have capacity for the second half of the year. Philippe Ombredanne welcomed this assistance and suggested integrating Jaime into the relevant Slack channels to facilitate synchronization with other maintainers.
- **VERS Progress and Collaboration**: Philippe Ombredanne admitted to delays in their progress on VERS due to competing demands. Jaime Rodríguez-Guerra offered to collaborate offline and act as an interface to help move these efforts forward.
- **Artificial Intelligence Policy**: Philippe Ombredanne noted that the team needs to discuss an Artificial Intelligence policy, as they are experimenting with deployment in the AboutCode ecosystem, and groups from Eclipse and ClearlyDefined have expressed interest.
- **Conda Prototype and Issue 868**: Discussion arose regarding recent activity on the Conda prototype. Jaime Rodríguez-Guerra and the team plan to standardize their approach and present a draft, with the work slated for the following week (see, e.g., https://github.com/package-url/purl-spec/issues/868).
- **Python Packaging Integration**: Jaime Rodríguez-Guerra discussed how the software-id initiative could support Python packaging by expressing dependencies external to PyPI, such as compilers and C++ libraries. They proposed exploring a middle ground where Python efforts could integrate with the registry, potentially by registering a specific chunk of the registry.
- **Virtual Dependencies**: The group explored the concept of "virtual" requirements—dependencies that are not satisfied by a single concrete package but by a set of them, such as interfaces or compilers. Philippe Ombredanne noted the similarity to the "provides" feature in Linux distributions like RPM or Debian. Michael Herzog suggested that virtual dependencies should be kept separate from the software-id initiative because they do not share the same attributes regarding specific versions and locations.
- **CPAN Registry Discussion**: Jon Moroney recalled a past discussion with Salve Nilsen regarding similar challenges for the CPAN registry and suggested involving them to find a common solution.
- **Pull Request Merging Order**: Philippe Ombredanne outlined a plan to merge Immanuel Kunz's pull request regarding canonical requirements first, followed by the pull request from Jaime Rodríguez-Guerra, to avoid merge conflicts.
- **VERS Specification Issue 51**: Immanuel Kunz confirmed that the work for issue 51 (https://github.com/package-url/vers-spec/issues/51) was implemented and agreed to close the issue.
- **URL Quoting in VERS**: Philippe Ombredanne addressed the requirement for URL quoting in versions, noting that while not common, it must be handled properly, and the group intends to review the documentation and test cases for this.
- **Private Namespaces in VERS**: Immanuel Kunz proposed allowing private namespaces in VERS using reverse DNS naming to avoid collisions. The group discussed the governance process for eventually promoting a private type to a registered type.
- **Provisional VERS Type Registration**: Philippe Ombredanne and Michael Herzog proposed a lightweight "provisional registration" process. Users could submit an issue template to reserve a name, ensuring that no one else uses it, without requiring the immediate complexity of full registration. This would allow for private use while preventing naming conflicts if the type later becomes public.
- **VERS 0.2 Schema Updates**: Michael Herzog announced plans to update the VERS test schema to v0.2. Changes include adopting "required" and "recommended" terminology for conformance, and using "validate" instead of "roundtrip".
- **PURL Specification Updates**: Michael Herzog reported that the PURL specification is accumulating a small number of updates for the ECMA-427 2nd Edition, primarily involving text corrections and the addition of enumerated values for namespaces and qualifiers to support recommended practices.
- **Versioning of Types**: Jonatan Männchen raised a question regarding the versioning of types, such as how to handle updates like Semantic Versioning 2 versus 1. The group concluded that they should avoid overcomplicating the structure and instead treat new versions as distinct types rather than versioning the types themselves.
- **License Versioning Process**: Michael Herzog discussed the discipline of versioning licenses as relevant to versioning VERS types, noting that many license authors do not anticipate the need for subsequent versions. If an author submits a license without versioning, the team is forced to manage it manually. However, if an author explicitly requests registration for a specific license version, the team accommodates it. The consensus was that this lack of initial versioning from authors should not derail the current processes.
- **PURL Adoption Metrics**: Philippe Ombredanne and Jon Moroney discussed the ongoing efforts to track PURL adoption within the CVE list, noting that providing accurate measures is difficult because not all CVEs are associated with packages. Jon referenced a conversation on the CVE mailing list where the initial report was that the current measurement is invalid because it does not account for proprietary software or software not distributed via package managers. The group discussed the need to arrive at a more realistic method for measuring adoption.
- **Associating PURL Information with CVEs**: Philippe Ombredanne inquired about the mechanism for submitting PURL data to CVE records. Jonatan Männchen clarified that they cannot submit pull requests directly to the CVE list repository. The group discussed potential paths, such as coordinating with every CNA or registering as an Authorized Data Provider (ADP) to supply additional information. Jon Moroney agreed to inquire about the specific requirements for this at the next meeting and to express the interest of the PURL community, while asking Philippe Ombredanne to reach out via Slack to coordinate.
- **Issues with the ADP Model**: Jon Moroney and Philippe Ombredanne discussed the drawbacks of the ADP model, noting that it creates a secondary information stream that can conflict with the primary CNA records. Jon cited an open issue, which was opened by Jerry Gamblin, highlighting that the Red Hat ADP disagrees with the Red Hat CNA. The participants agreed that this model effectively creates a new, potentially confusing subsection in CVE records, leading to a "consumer to origin to secondary origin" model that becomes unmanageable.
- **Strategic Focus on 'software-id' and 'git' PURL Types**: Jonatan Männchen emphasized that a primary goal is the release of software-id, as its current unavailability prevents widespread adoption despite the existence of perfect use cases. Jon Moroney added that the team is also prioritizing Git types because nearly everyone utilizes Git-based source code, regardless of their specific package registry usage. The participants agreed that these are essential focus areas.
- **Status of the ADP Pilot Program**: Michael Herzog noted that information on the organization's website indicates the ADP pilot program is scheduled to run through the current month, with its extension remaining uncertain. The group acknowledged that the pilot does not appear to have been a resounding success and agreed to await further information regarding its status.
- The meeting was adjourned.
